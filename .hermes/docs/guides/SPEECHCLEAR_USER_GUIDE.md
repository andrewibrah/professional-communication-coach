# SpeechClear: How to Practice and See Whether You Speak More Clearly

A step-by-step guide for people who want better conversations—not technical instructions.

## Start here

SpeechClear helps you practice professional communication: introducing yourself, answering interview questions, explaining a technical issue, or responding to a customer. The useful cycle is simple:

**Speak → review one correction → try again → use the improvement without reading a script.**

You do not need to understand how the app works behind the scenes. You need a microphone, somewhere quiet, and a real situation you want to handle better.

> **Current version:** SpeechClear is a private alpha—an early version. Guided Voice has been implemented, but its complete real-person voice experience has not yet been accepted through live testing. Treat this guide as instructions for your own evaluation, not a promise that every feature already works flawlessly. A connection problem is an app issue, not evidence that you are a poor speaker.

## 1. Pick the right kind of practice

| Mode | What you do | Best reason to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Recorded Practice** | Give a complete answer lasting 30–180 seconds, submit it, and read a coaching report. | Establish a starting point, improve your own answer, and compare attempts. |
| **Guided Voice** | Listen to a short spoken exercise, repeat it when it is your turn, and receive brief coaching before retrying or moving on. | Practice useful wording in small steps, including listening without reading. |

Guided Voice is a short, structured exercise—not an unlimited free-form conversation. Recorded Practice is where you build a longer answer in your own words.

**Recommended first visit:** Make one Recorded Practice attempt before looking at an example. Use its main correction to guide a retry. Then try Guided Voice. Finish with another answer in your own words.

## 2. What you need before starting

- Open the web address supplied by the person running SpeechClear. For the current local setup, that address is **http://localhost:5173** on the computer running the app. It is not a public website address that automatically works on another device.
- Use a desktop or laptop browser with microphone access. The current interface is designed around desktop use; do not assume every phone or browser has been verified.
- Use a quiet room and a working microphone. Headphones can keep the coach's voice from playing back into your microphone.
- Make sure you can hear normal computer audio. Guided Voice needs both a microphone and sound output.
- Have internet access and a verified account.
- Use only made-up or nonconfidential work examples. Do not say passwords, real customer records, sensitive employer information, or private account details.

If the page will not open, ask the person running the app to start it. You should not need to install software or enter technical commands.

## 3. Sign in and verify your account

1. Click **Sign in** near the top of the page.
2. If you already have an account, enter your email and password, then click **Sign in securely**.
3. If you need an account, click **Create an account**, enter your details, and choose **Create account**. The form requires a password of at least eight characters.
4. Check your email and follow the account-verification instructions.
5. Return to SpeechClear and sign in if needed.

Email verification is required for AI-powered practice. Being able to browse scenario cards does not mean you are signed in or ready to practice.

Other options in the sign-in window:

- **Forgot password?** sends a password-reset request.
- **Email me a sign-in link** lets an existing account request a link instead of using a password.
- If an email does not arrive, check spam and confirm the address. If the link fails or account setup is unavailable, ask the person running the app for help.

Do not share passwords or sign-in links in your test notes.

## 4. Find your way around

The left-hand navigation has five main areas:

- **Overview:** starting point, scenario entry, and a summary of Recorded Practice activity and scores.
- **Practice studio:** choose a scenario and switch between Recorded Practice and Guided Voice.
- **Practice history:** reopen recorded sessions and review saved guided sessions.
- **Progress:** view the latest Recorded Practice score for each scored session over time.
- **Profile & privacy:** save useful professional context, see the displayed practice usage, and manage available deletion controls.

**Important:** Leaving the Practice studio, switching away from Guided Voice, or signing out ends the current guided connection. Do not use navigation as a way to keep a live coach waiting in the background.

## 5. Add a little professional context

Open **Profile & privacy**. You can provide your current or target role, industry, experience level, communication goal, preferred tone, audience, and what you want to improve. Click **Save profile** after making changes.

You do not need to write a résumé. Short, useful descriptions are enough:

| Field | Example you can adapt |
|---|---|
| Current or target role | IT support technician |
| Experience level | Entry level |
| Primary communication goal | Explain problems clearly without rambling |
| Preferred tone | Calm, professional, and natural |
| What would you like to improve? | I give too much background before the main point |
| Your audience | Customers who do not work in technology |

These are example inputs, not results produced by the app. Keep personal and confidential details out of the profile.

## 6. Choose a scenario and one goal

Open **Practice studio** and choose a conversation:

| Scenario | What to practice |
|---|---|
| **Tell me about yourself** | A focused introduction relevant to your audience. |
| **Technical job interview** | Explaining your approach and a useful example. |
| **Help-desk troubleshooting** | Acknowledging the problem and giving a practical next step. |
| **Explain cybersecurity** | Making a complex topic understandable. |
| **Sales discovery & objections** | Understanding the concern and responding with relevance. |
| **Customer escalation** | Acknowledging frustration and setting realistic expectations. |

Select a **Communication goal**:

- Be clear and concise
- Structure my answer
- Explain complex ideas simply
- Sound professional and natural
- Respond with empathy

Use **Additional context** only when it helps. Example: “I am explaining a connection problem to a customer who is frustrated and unfamiliar with technology.”

For your first comparison, keep the scenario, goal, and context the same. Changing all three makes it harder to tell what improved.

## 7. Recorded Practice: make your first attempt

### Prepare the question

1. Choose **Recorded Practice** in the Practice studio.
2. Select your scenario, goal, and optional context.
3. Click **Generate my scenario**.
4. Read the generated question and context.
5. For a fair starting point, leave **Read a strong example response** closed until after your first attempt.

A useful answer shape is: **main point → example or explanation → next step**. Do not memorize a complete answer before your first attempt; you want an honest starting point.

### Record and submit

1. Click **Start recording**.
2. Allow microphone access if your browser asks.
3. Speak naturally for **at least 30 seconds and no more than three minutes**. Do not add empty words just to make the answer longer; include an actual explanation or example.
4. Click **Finish recording** when you are done. Recording stops automatically at three minutes.
5. Click **Get my feedback** to submit the response.
6. Wait for the upload and coaching report to finish.

There is no recording pause mode. Use **Cancel recording** to cancel, or **Discard & record again** after recording if you do not want to submit that response.

Until you submit, the recording is held in the current browser tab. Closing or leaving the tab can lose that unsubmitted attempt. The app does not provide a saved audio replay library.

### Read the report in the right order

Start with these four parts rather than trying to fix everything:

1. **Your full transcript:** Did the written version capture what you actually said?
2. **Your priority improvement:** What is the one change to make next?
3. **Evidence from your response:** Does the quoted wording support that correction?
4. **Your next practice exercise:** What action can you take on the next attempt?

Then look at **What worked**, **Communication breakdown**, **A more effective answer**, and **Apply in your next conversation**.

The report can also flag filler words, jargon, weak phrasing, missed questions, and pacing observations. Treat these as suggestions to inspect, not unquestionable measurements. The feedback is based on the transcribed answer; it is not a reliable measurement of pronunciation, accent, pitch, vocal confidence, or exact speaking speed. A transcription mistake can produce an unfair correction.

### Try again on the same question

1. Choose one correction you agree with.
2. Write a short reminder, such as “Answer first, background second.”
3. Click **Try again in this session**.
4. Record another complete answer and submit it.
5. Compare the displayed **First attempt** and **Latest attempt** reports.

Use the improved example for structure, not as a script. A better result is an answer you can reproduce naturally—not a perfect reading of the AI's wording.

## 8. Guided Voice: practice short spoken exercises

### Start the session

1. Open **Practice studio** and choose **Guided Voice**.
2. Select a scenario, communication goal, and optional context.
3. For your first visit, keep the prompt visible so you can understand the sequence.
4. Click **Start guided session**.
5. Allow microphone access if asked and wait for the connection.
6. Listen to the coach's short exercise.
7. Wait until the screen says **Your turn — microphone enabled after playback settles** before repeating it.

Repeat the short target sentence as instructed. Do not give a long interview answer unless the exercise explicitly asks for it. The current guided flow prepares three short exercises, with a limited number of attempts rather than endless retries. A session has an overall limit of up to five minutes; the countdown on screen is your guide.

Some scenario cards still display “30–180 sec.” That is the Recorded Practice answer length, not a requirement to speak for that long in a guided exercise.

### Understand the session messages

| Message | What you should do |
|---|---|
| **Connecting** | Wait; do not start talking yet. |
| **Coach speaking — microphone off** | Listen to the entire exercise or correction. |
| **Your turn — microphone enabled after playback settles** | Speak your response clearly. |
| **Reviewing your attempt — microphone off** | Wait for the correction or next exercise. |
| **Paused — microphone released** | Use Resume if you want to continue soon. |
| **Reconnecting** | Wait for a connection update before speaking. |
| **Session finished** | Review the recap or set up another session. |
| **Session ended with an error** | Note the message and check saved history; do not assume unsaved speech was retained. |

The app can recognize the end of an attempt. **I’m done** is also available during your listening turn if you want to signal you have finished speaking. It is different from **End session**.

### Use the controls

| Control | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Replay** | Hear the current exercise again. It is not a new scored attempt. |
| **Try again** | Request another attempt on the current exercise when retries remain. |
| **I’m done** | Signal that your current response is complete while it is your turn. |
| **Mute microphone / Unmute microphone** | Stop or allow microphone input during eligible session states. |
| **Pause / Resume** | Take a brief break and continue if the session is still eligible. |
| **End session** | Finish the guided session rather than completing another attempt. |
| **Reconnect** | Attempt recovery when the app exposes this button after a disconnection. |
| **Enable coach audio** | Allow playback when your browser has blocked the coach's audio. |

A disabled button often means the app is connecting, speaking, reviewing, paused, disconnected, or out of retries. Wait for the appropriate state rather than clicking repeatedly.

Pause and mute do not give you unlimited time. The overall countdown continues, and a long pause or lost connection can end the session. End the session if you need a longer break.

### Read the short correction

When visible, **Latest feedback** may include:

- **Strength:** something useful in the response.
- **Priority correction:** the main adjustment to make.
- **Corrected example:** wording to practice.
- **Transcript evidence:** the words supporting the feedback.
- A direction such as **Try this exercise again**, **Next exercise**, or **Practice complete**.

If the transcript is uncertain, the app should not give a confident assessment. That means it needs clearer input—not that your communication ability is poor.

Corrections assess the recognized wording. They do not establish whether your pronunciation, accent, or vocal delivery improved.

## 9. Show Prompt and Hide Prompt

Use the visible prompt first to understand an exercise. Then use hidden practice to see whether you can listen and respond without reading.

- **Hide Prompt** removes the target wording from the current practice display. It also hides the latest transcript and detailed feedback that could give away the wording.
- **Show Prompt** reveals the text and detailed feedback again.
- You can choose visibility before starting or change it during a session.
- Changing visibility is not a scored attempt. It should not change the exercise or reset your progress.
- **Replay** is useful if the prompt is hidden and you need to hear it again.

Hiding the prompt is a practice aid, **not a privacy or deletion setting**. Saved prompts, transcripts, and feedback still exist. After a session ends, **Review guided session** in history displays saved exercise text and feedback—even if you practiced with the prompt hidden.

For a useful self-check, ask: “Can I repeat the meaning and wording without relying on the screen?” Success here demonstrates performance on this drill. It does not, by itself, prove you can handle a new conversation.

## 10. A simple before-and-after routine

Use this routine to see whether the coaching transfers into your own speech. It is a suggested practice method, not an automatic app feature.

### A. Establish your starting point

- Choose a situation you actually care about.
- Make a Recorded Practice attempt before opening the example response.
- Check the transcript and record the main correction in your own notes.
- Identify one observable problem: too much background, unexplained jargon, missing next step, or not acknowledging the customer's concern.

### B. Practice one improvement

- Make a retry in the same recorded session, focusing only on that correction.
- If useful, do a Guided Voice session with the same scenario and goal.
- Start with visible text. Try hidden listening practice once you understand the flow.

### C. Test the improvement without a script

- Return to your recorded session through **Practice history → Open**.
- Choose **Try again in this session**.
- Answer without reading the example or a prepared script.
- Compare your first and latest transcripts and reports.

For a stronger check later, try a different question in the same scenario. Ask a trusted person whether your answer makes sense and what they would do next. Do not call a memorized retry proof of general improvement.

### D. Judge the words, not only the score

| Question | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| Did I answer the question earlier? | The opening states the main point instead of a long preamble. |
| Is my answer easier to follow? | It has a clear order and a relevant example. |
| Would this audience understand it? | Necessary technical terms are explained. |
| Did I respond to the concern? | I acknowledge what matters to the listener. |
| Is there a practical next step? | The listener knows what happens next. |
| Can I do it without reading? | I can give a fresh answer in my own words. |

The **/100 score is AI feedback on an attempt, not a grade for your personal ability**. A higher score alone is not proof of improvement. More convincing evidence is a visible wording change that you agree is useful and can reproduce in a different conversation.

The **Progress** page uses the latest report per recorded session. It is not a chart of every retry and does not include Guided Voice as a numeric score. An improved retry can change that session's latest score, so keep your own comparison notes if you want a clear baseline.

### Copyable practice notes

Keep these somewhere private. You do not need to paste full transcripts into a bug report.

```text
Date:
Scenario and goal:
The situation I want to handle better:

BEFORE
Main point of my first answer:
One weakness I agree with:
A short, nonconfidential example of that weakness:
First score, if useful:

PRACTICE
One correction I worked on:
What I tried differently:
Did I rely on a visible prompt or example?

AFTER
Latest score, if useful:
A wording change that genuinely helped:
Can I explain the idea without reading?
What still needs work?
One thing to use in my next real conversation:
```

## 11. Review saved work

Open **Practice history**.

### Recorded sessions

Click **Open** beside the recorded session you want to review. You can return to its question, first/latest reports, and another retry. The transcript is available inside the report.

### Guided sessions

Scroll to **Guided Voice history**. Finished sessions offer **Review guided session**. You can see saved exercises, attempts, feedback, and any available recap. Use **Close guided review** when done.

An interrupted session may have fewer saved attempts than you expected. Speech that was not finalized and saved is not guaranteed to appear. A recap describes saved work; it is not a claim that every exercise was completed successfully.

## 12. Privacy, deletion, and limits

- Do not use confidential material for testing. AI providers receive content needed to generate exercises and assess responses.
- Recorded Practice audio is sent for processing and is scheduled for deletion after processing. Failures or outages can delay cleanup. Saved transcripts and reports remain until deleted; recorded audio replay is not stored.
- Guided Voice sends microphone audio to the voice provider while listening. The app saves prompts, transcripts, feedback, and a recap, but does not retain raw guided audio. Provider retention policies still apply.
- Use **Delete** beside a particular recorded or guided session to remove that session's saved content. Read the confirmation carefully: deletion cannot be undone.
- For Guided Voice, use its dedicated history Delete control. Do not assume the **Delete all history** control clears both modes; it is wired to the recorded-history flow.
- Account deletion is currently marked **Integration pending** and is not available in the interface.
- **Practice usage** shows the displayed AI-operation allowance. Creating scenarios and processing recorded responses use operations. Guided Voice also has enforced duration, retry, and spending limits; the usage panel is not a complete guided cost meter.
- If a limit is reached, respect the message. Do not repeatedly start new sessions or create another account to get around it. Ask the person running the app when practice will be available again.

## 13. If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| The page does not open | Confirm the supplied address. For localhost, ask the person running the app to start the local services. |
| You see an offline preview or service-unavailable message | You can browse descriptions, but do not expect real generated practice. Report the message. |
| Start is disabled | Choose a scenario, verify your account, and check whether the mode is available. |
| Microphone access was denied | Use the browser's site-permission controls to allow the microphone, then try again. Check your computer's microphone permission too if needed. |
| You cannot hear the coach | Check volume and output device. Click Enable coach audio if shown. Do not answer a prompt you never heard. |
| Your speech is missing or badly transcribed | Wait for Your turn, check that you are unmuted, and reduce background noise. Note the mismatch rather than trusting its feedback. |
| Recorded response is too short | Make a new recording of at least 30 seconds. Include a useful example rather than empty filler. |
| Upload or feedback fails | Read the error. If Get my feedback remains available, retry once; if a new recording is required, record again. Check history before creating duplicate sessions. |
| Guided connection drops | Wait for the state update. Use Reconnect if offered. Check saved history if recovery fails; unfinished speech may be lost. |
| The session ends during a break | Pause and overall limits still apply. Start another session when ready. |
| A correction does not match what you said | Inspect the transcript and evidence. Record the discrepancy; do not accept the correction automatically. |
| History fails to load | Return to the history page to retry. If deletion was not confirmed, reload history before assuming it succeeded. |
| You reach a practice limit | Stop and follow the message or ask when the allowance resets. |

If a problem repeats, stop rather than spending your whole practice session reconnecting. Report:

```text
Mode and scenario:
What I clicked:
What I expected:
What actually happened:
Exact error message, if any:
Could I hear the coach?
Was the microphone allowed and unmuted?
Browser and device:
Did anything appear in saved history?
```

Do not include passwords, sign-in links, private recordings, or confidential transcripts. Crop screenshots to the relevant message.

## 14. First-visit checklist

- [ ] I signed in with a verified account.
- [ ] I chose one scenario and one goal.
- [ ] I recorded a natural starting answer before reading an example.
- [ ] I checked whether the transcript was accurate.
- [ ] I chose one useful correction and retried the same question.
- [ ] If Guided Voice was available, I listened before speaking and used the correct turn state.
- [ ] I tried hidden practice only after understanding the exercise.
- [ ] I gave a fresh answer without reading a script.
- [ ] I identified one concrete improvement—or honestly recorded that I have not seen one yet.
- [ ] I ended the guided session before leaving and checked saved history.

**Your goal is not to impress the AI. Your goal is to make the next conversation easier for the person listening.**
